## 1. Chosen Niche

**Target Role: Full-Stack Developer (JavaScript/TypeScript-centered, React + Node.js)**

I chose Full-Stack Development because it directly matches the bootcamp curriculum and offers the widest range of entry points into the industry. Rather than specializing narrowly on day one, a full-stack foundation lets me work across the entire product: frontend UI, backend APIs, and the database layer which is exactly what most small-to-mid-size companies (the type most likely to hire a junior developer) actually need. It also keeps more doors open: once I have a full-stack foundation, I can specialize later into frontend, backend, or DevOps based on what I enjoy and where the market moves.

Within full-stack, I focused specifically on the JavaScript/TypeScript ecosystem (React on the frontend, Node.js on the backend) because it appeared as the dominant stack across the listings I researched. I intentionally researched listings across Lebanon, Dubai (UAE), and the United States (remote) to see whether the target skill set changes by region or holds steady - more on that in `market-research.md`.

## Final Reflection

Going into this research, I expected the target stack to shift noticeably depending on region - that Dubai's more startup-heavy market would look very different from Lebanon's or the US's. What I actually found was more grounded: React and Node.js dominate everywhere, regardless of whether the employer is a Lebanese system integrator, a Dubai product startup, or a US government contractor. What did shift regionally was the secondary tooling layered on top of that core, Dubai listings leaned toward newer product tooling (Next.js, Tailwind, MERN-specific stacks), while Lebanon and US enterprise/government listings leaned toward more traditional Java/Spring + Angular combinations. AWS came up consistently across all three regions, confirming that basic cloud literacy isn't optional no matter where I end up working. This reshaped my roadmap in one meaningful way: I'm prioritizing depth in React + Node.js + TypeScript first since that transfers across every market I researched, and treating region-specific tooling (Next.js for more startup/product-style employers, or Java/Spring/Angular for enterprise and government-style employers) as an optional specialization layer to add later depending on which type of employer I end up targeting.
